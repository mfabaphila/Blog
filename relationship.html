5 Relationship Lessons

Please don’t quote me on this, but I am sure there's a common belief that Romeo and Juliet is universally regarded as the most romantic book, and Titanic as the most romantic movie ever made. Ironically, both tales end in tragedy.

It's a reminder that love is a game that none of us gets to win. You can still enjoy the game though—even if you are heading towards a giant iceberg or you might soon slice yourself with Romeo’s dagger.

As someone who's retired from the dating scene, or a "datist" as some might say, I've learned from my fair share of failures in relationships. I've witnessed more relationship failures than even the combined struggles of Chelsea FC and Kaizer Chiefs.

Despite my personal setbacks, I've gained valuable insights. If your goal is to have the best relationship in 2024, here are some lessons that might just guide you through the maze:

1. Do you know how farmers separate the wheat from the chaff? Or how they do not place two bulls in the same barn? Be a good farmer; KEEP YOUR FRIENDS AWAY FROM YOUR RELATIONSHIP. Winnow them well and keep them far.

This is not because they are bad friends who intentionally try to sabotage your new relationship. The partner–friend relationship is something close to the Oedipus complex. Your friends want you as much as they have you. When you are no longer as available as you used to be, guess who gets the blame?

I am not saying hide your partner from your friends; I am suggesting that you be like chicken pox—bring him or her around once in a blue moon.

2. I think this one should be on a bumper sticker, a jumbotron, or a Mr Price T-shirt. DON’T HOLD BACK. Getting into a new relationship cautiously is like smelling someone's food after they have already dished for you.

Someone gets offended, then retaliates. The vibration of doubt is easily transferred to the next person. If you start doubtfully, I will see you and raise you a double. Two years later, you will still call each other "someone I know."

If you are worried about getting hurt, don’t. Heartbreaks are inevitable. One way or another, you will experience one. The maxim, "Better to have loved and lost than never to have loved at all," speaks directly to this point.

3. From the classical to the medieval period, women stayed home while men went to seek gold. Later, women demanded the same opportunities as men, and for that we are grateful to Mary Wollstonecraft.

That 50/50 eventually included everything except money. Men were still expected to spend, even when women earned. This gave birth to the modern slay queen.

Chances are, you will date one. When you do, for the sake of your financial future, SPEAK FORWARD. Money conversations are uncomfortable, but they save relationships. Set time aside, discuss finances honestly, reach an agreement, and move on.

4. The first sign of sanity is admitting that you are insane. NEVER START A NEW RELATIONSHIP PRETENDING THAT YOU ARE PERFECT.

There is nothing harder than maintaining a false identity. Put your cards on the table. Share the good, the bad, and the ugly. A relationship is not a job interview.

No, Sean, you cannot work under pressure. Your weakness is probably not that you work too hard or cannot say no to people. You are probably a stoner, a porn lover, or maybe you only bathe occasionally (guilty of the last one). Let it be known. Be your authentic self.

5. Babies' common first words are "hi," "bhabha," and "mama." Your first common words in a relationship should be, "I am sorry, I was wrong."

Those words will save you countless unnecessary conflicts, arguments, and headaches. That said, you should occasionally put a "fuck off" where it is due. If you are always the apologetic Samaritan, you will get played. Big time.

Find a balance. A polite, humble, and mature balance.
