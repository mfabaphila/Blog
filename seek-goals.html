Don't Seek Goals, Seek Yourself

Before I delve into my usual musings, let me assign you your first homework for the year. Can you list your top five goals for 2024?

There is a famous maxim that says, “Birds of the same feather flock together.” Not only have I heard this saying, but I have also witnessed a gaggle meandering through thin blades of cirrus clouds and uniformly landing on a marshy pond to feed on water lilies.

Likewise, people flock with those whom they resonate with the most. This is because we belong to cultures, families, and communities. No man is an island.

The good thing about being part of a group is that when the lion attacks, you can collectively defend yourself against it and enjoy its meat around the fire where the sage of the group will be telling educational folklore.

The downside is that vegans and vegetarians cannot excuse themselves. This is because the norms of the group mask the individuality in you. In other words, popularity is a direct product of social unity. We do it because this is what is done here.

When you seek goals, you are more likely to follow the flock. This is because each group, culture, or society defines what should be considered acceptable goals.

I am not saying they are wrong; I am saying they might not be yours—they might not be you. I suggest that the only way to know what is uniquely you and yours is for you to find yourself.

The moment you start seeking goals, what will come first in your mind is what is familiar to you, which is what the flock around you has been preaching.

I can list no more than ten of those goals, and I can guarantee that if you completed the task above, you have more than two of them on your list.

Here is the list of those socially acceptable goals: financial freedom, mental and physical health, promotion or a new job, education, and relationships.

Again, there is absolutely nothing wrong with these goals, but it is very crucial that you find yourself first. You might discover that you love petting a dog more than being a millionaire.

If you have already started the goals list, I suggest tearing it apart. Use that paper to start your braai fire next weekend. Keep a sharp eye on my blog; I will advise you on how to find yourself.
