The Boken Healer-Phila Mfaba

Say it With Your Heart

In the memorial film The Great Dictator, the legendary comedian Charlie Chaplin ends the film with a heartfelt speech condemning the popular obsession of prioritizing thinking over feeling. The gist of the speech is that humankind has evolved to operate everything on the intellectual capacity and forget about emotional intelligence, leading to more harm, pain, and suffering.

Greed has poisoned men’s souls, has barricaded the world with hate, has goose-stepped us into misery and bloodshed. We have developed speed, but we have shut ourselves in. Machinery that gives abundance has left us in want. Our knowledge has made us cynical. Our cleverness, hard and unkind. We think too much and feel too little. More than machinery we need humanity. More than cleverness we need kindness and gentleness. Without these qualities, life will be violent and all will be lost… (The Great Dictator, 1940)

In this speech, Hynkel (Charlie Chaplin) expresses how overthinking leads to all sorts of violence and hatred. In this article, I want to discuss how the mind leads to division through binary identification. Like most of my opinions in self-development, I always drag my foot as if I am testing the temperature of hot water so that when I mature just a little bit, I will not be ashamed to criticize my younger self.

The point that I am making here is that when it comes to dealing and cooperating with people, you search for the heart; it will give you the right direction. What I have observed, however, is that most people (myself included) operate our relationships with the mind. The rationale between friends and foes; us and them; black and white; right and wrong; left and right is the result of rationalizing—using the mind instead of the heart.

I am not advocating, however, that the mind is futile, and I don’t think that when Charlie Chaplin said "We think too much and feel too little" he was condemning thinking altogether. However, when it comes to people, the heart will do a much better job than the mind can do. The difference is like day and night. Wherever you see love, happiness, understanding, joy, or cooperation you can be assured that the heart was at play. Think about a mother's unconditional love; no reasoning or justification can change their love because it is heartfelt.

The heart has the wisdom to understand that everything is impersonal. That all human beings and their behaviors are all to be viewed in a non-binary way. The heart has no hate; it is the mind that negotiates hate on behalf of the heart. This is because our mind lives in a socially constructed world, with preferences, choices, sides, and other binary identifications that we use to distinguish us from them.

On the other hand, the heart is whole. It sees no sides; it sees everything as complete. It takes no sides because it does not create any binaries. To the heart, the world and its people are all perfect as they are. To the mind, there is them and us. They need to join our team and if not, we are mortal enemies. Fights, disputes, divorces, wars, riots, strikes, lawsuits, and more are all the results of the binary thinking created by the mind that prefers this over that and them over us.

The mind has its place. Without the mind, for example, we would not have witnessed the medieval era that introduced spectacles and the Renaissance era that gave us the printing press that made it possible for you to read this article, or use it in the loo before your first flush. All kinds of civilizations that we love and enjoy are all fruits of the mind. The mind is a great creator of things but a worse weapon for building relationships.

If there was an imaginary world where people didn’t have reasoning capacity, there would be 100% peace—no conflicts, hate, fights, disputes, and all. That is because there is no room for rationalizing behavior, looks, style, preferences, personalities, and choices. The heart enters the room of diverse characters and accepts all with open arms until the mind convinces it to choose that character and disown the other character. When Charlie Chaplin said "We think too much and feel too little," he meant…

Thinking puts you in a room; Carlos Castaneda makes this analogy of how we use our conscious thinking from birth. We are placed in a mansion of about 1000 rooms, and through our thinking and observing and mimicking and modeling and confirming we all end up using only one room; the only way thinkers leave the room is by dying. What takes you out of the one room to the other 999 rooms is the heart. It knows no boundaries and no limitations because it does not label. If you stay with your heart long enough, you will soon be able to get out of the house without having to die first.
